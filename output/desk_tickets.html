Problem 10 · Post-run analysis

What the team did well—and what it could not do

A review grounded in our agent app, the three Problem 9 run records, and the Campus Customs database.

3 runs reviewed

I evaluated the workflow state separately from the real-world outcome. The API marked each ticket resolved when the Boss returned a validated response; that status means the agent review finished. It does not mean a tee was delivered, rent was paid, or a discount/order was approved. All three runs used the local PydanticAI FunctionModel and database-backed MCP server, so no Portkey credits were used.

1. Agent performance on each ticket

Ticket 101 · Good triage, incomplete fulfillment

Bulldog tee stockout

The Boss followed the Problem 6 plan closely: it delegated first to Inventory, then Accounting. Inventory confirmed zero CC-TEE-WHITE size-S units; Accounting surfaced invoice 501 ($840, open, due 2026-08-28) and Bulldog Print Co’s recorded five-day lead time.

Why that was useful: The team found both the immediate blocker and a plausible linked reprint record without telling Tauhid that stock or a delivery date was guaranteed.

What fell short: The agents could not verify that the invoice represents a currently active production order, contact the vendor, reserve a tee, or update inventory. The Boss’s returned summary was generic, so the evidence carried the useful detail, not the prose recommendation.

Ticket 102 · Strongest plan-to-run match

Rent due

The Boss delegated to Facilities first and Accounting next, exactly as expected. Facilities checked lease 1: $2,400 monthly rent due 2026-09-02 to Elm City Properties, which matches the email’s “in two days” against desk date 2026-08-31. Accounting found a $3,400 checking snapshot and no payment rows.

Why that was useful: It corroborated the notice and checked whether there was a recorded payment before taking another step.

What fell short: No payment was authorized or recorded, and the account snapshot alone does not confirm that funds are still available in an external bank. A human must authorize and reconcile any rent payment.

Ticket 103 · Correct delegation, decision intentionally held

Bulk hoodie discount

The Boss consulted Inventory, Accounting, and Customer Service, matching the planned sequence. Inventory found 8 size-M hoodies for a request of 20 (a shortage of 12). Accounting retrieved the $58 list price. Customer Service used its restricted, customer-safe order context.

Why that was useful: The team stated the shortage and price evidence without exposing internal unit cost to customer-facing context or inventing a discount rule.

What fell short: It did not produce a concrete discount recommendation, create a purchase order for the missing 12, or send a customer response. Those actions need policy, purchasing, and communication support.

Evaluation caveat: The local mock correctly exercised Boss delegation, role-filtered MCP access, database evidence, validated replies, and append-only graph events. But the mock currently returns nearly identical generic summaries and recommendations for every role. Thus, strong evidence gathering and correct delegation are visible; nuanced reasoning quality is not demonstrated by these local-mock outputs.

3. What would be simpler with a single agent and tools?

These are three fixed, narrow requests against a small database. One ticket-aware agent with a read-only toolset could select the relevant combined lookup, gather the result, and return a structured response. That would remove the Boss-to-specialist handoffs, repeated prompt/model cycles, duplicated generic summaries, and some audit volume. It would also simplify testing: one run per ticket and one response schema to inspect. That is especially attractive in local mock mode, where all role outputs currently use the same generic template.

The tradeoff is access control and independent review. The five-role setup gives Customer Service a narrower view than Accounting and creates a clear Facilities/Inventory specialty boundary. If one agent received every tool, it would be easier to expose internal cost or payment context to a customer-facing answer, and there would be less role-level accountability. A practical middle ground is one orchestrator with specialist tool permissions or explicit, small workflows, rather than asking every agent to weigh in on every ticket.

4. Three new problems the current team and tools can handle

  1. Check a different recorded item/size before answering a stock question. Inventory can query an existing SKU and size through lookup_inventory(sku, size); Customer Service can use the customer-safe order context when the request is attached to a supported ticket. The result is recorded on-hand stock, not a reservation or delivery promise.
  2. Review the status and vendor context for invoice 501. Accounting can use get_invoice_context(invoice_id=501) to retrieve the invoice, vendor, cash snapshot, and payment rows and report that the record is open/overdue. It can recommend a human reconciliation; it cannot pay the vendor or mark the invoice paid.
  3. Check whether a rent notice agrees with the recorded lease. Facilities and Accounting can use get_lease_context(lease_id=1) to compare the lease due date and rent with the cash snapshot and recorded payments. This is a useful verification workflow, while an actual payment still requires a human decision.

5. Three problems the current setup cannot solve

Place or receive a replenishment order

For ticket 101 or 103, the app can report stock and vendor facts but cannot confirm production, obtain a quote, place a purchase order, track shipment, or increase inventory on receipt.

Add: a Procurement/Receiving agent; vendor catalog and purchase-order tools; shipment tracking; a receiving and inventory-adjustment tool with approval, idempotency, and audit controls.

Send and track customer or vendor communications

The Customer Service role can inspect safe records but there is no CRM, order-management, email, or messaging integration. It cannot send a tailored availability update or follow up about the reprint.

Add: a Customer Operations agent plus customer/order lookup and message-drafting tools. Sending should require an explicit human review/approval and record delivery status.

Execute and reconcile real payments or discounts

POST /approvals changes the local cash ledger and appends a payment row; it does not move bank funds, mark an invoice/lease paid, reconcile identifiers, or apply a discount policy. A $3,400 snapshot is not bank authorization.

Add: a Finance/Payments agent, a payment-provider/AP integration, a formal invoice-to-payment reconciliation model, and a versioned discount-policy/approval tool. Require a named human approver before any financial commit.

6. Overall assessment and next improvements

The team demonstrated useful, grounded triage: it fetched relevant rows, delegated the specialties anticipated in the desk plans, respected the customer-safe data boundary, and left financial/policy decisions unapproved. The three workflow statuses and append-only audit entries make the run visible in the dashboard. The cash ledger stayed at $3,400 because no approval was submitted.

  • Improve the local mock so each role’s final summary and recommendation reflect its returned records rather than the same generic text.
  • Keep the tool names and run correlation IDs visible in the audit feed so future plan-versus-actual reviews can verify each call directly.
  • Separate “review completed” from “business issue fulfilled” in ticket status; current backend marks a ticket resolved whenever the agent run succeeds, even when the order, rent, or discount decision still needs a human.
  • Add approved, narrowly scoped write tools only after a human-reviewed workflow exists for purchases, invoice reconciliation, customer messages, and stock receipt.
